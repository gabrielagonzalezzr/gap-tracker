Five case studies, side by side

Same scoring model applied to five very different interventions. The point isn't to rank which cause matters more — it's to see which gaps are best understood and most actionable right now.

Case studyAdoption gapEffect magnitudeEvidence ageFriction clarity

School meals and legal services score highest on every factor — large, clearly measured effects, long-documented evidence, and a well-understood single mechanism. These are the clearest cases of "we know the fix, it's just not adopted broadly enough."

Digital finance and youth training sit in the middle: real, meaningful gaps, but either the mechanism is more compound (several overlapping frictions) or the evidence base is newer.

Reentry employment scores lowest, deliberately. Its effect and friction scores are low not because the issue matters less, but because the research itself hasn't confirmed a clear causal fix yet — a genuinely different kind of gap than the other four.
